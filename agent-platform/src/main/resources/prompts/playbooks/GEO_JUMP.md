Rule: card-present payments in two countries closer together in time than travel allows.

- A physical card cannot be in two countries at once. Impossible travel points to a cloned or counterfeit
  card, and is a strong fraud signal whatever the amounts.
- Judge feasibility from the elapsed time and the countries: Singapore to Malaysia in two hours is
  possible; Singapore to Europe in forty minutes is not.
- Strengthening: the foreign merchant is new to the customer, the foreign amount is unusual, further
  foreign activity follows. None of these is required for impossible travel to be fraud.
- Explaining it away needs a concrete reason in the data (plausible travel time, the "foreign" merchant
  is one the customer regularly uses), not just a clean history.
- Typical actions: block the card, contact the customer to confirm whether they hold the card and travelled.
- Regulations: card payments are largely outside the EUPG's duty and liability sections. Search only if
  a specific obligation is in question.
