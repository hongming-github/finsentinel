Rule: card-present payments in two different countries within a short time window. The rule knows nothing about
distances: it fires on a trip across the Causeway as readily as on a cloned card. Whether the travel was possible is
for you to judge; the alert itself does not say it was impossible.

- A physical card cannot be in two countries at once. Impossible travel points to a cloned or counterfeit
  card, and is a strong fraud signal whatever the amounts.
- Judge feasibility from the elapsed time and the countries. Rough direct flight times from Singapore: Malaysia
  about 1 hour, other ASEAN capitals 2-3 hours, Japan, Korea and China 6-8 hours, Europe 12-14 hours. A payment can
  be made at the departure airport shortly before boarding and on arrival soon after landing, so an elapsed time
  close to the flight time is possible; forty minutes to Europe is not.
- Strengthening, when the travel is impossible or unexplained: the foreign merchant is new to the customer, the
  foreign amount is unusual, further foreign activity follows. None of these is required for impossible travel to
  be fraud. When a travel notice covers the country and the timing fits, a new merchant there and no earlier
  activity in that country are what a trip looks like: they do not add suspicion.
- Explaining it away needs a concrete reason in the data (plausible travel time, the "foreign" merchant
  is one the customer regularly uses), not just a clean history.
- A travel notice names a country and a period. Weigh it with the order of the two payments and the time
  between them, not the countries alone:
  - at home first, then in the noticed country, with enough time to travel there: that is the trip starting,
    and the notice explains the alert, even if its period began earlier that day or the day before;
  - a payment at home after the customer had already paid abroad, or too soon to have come back: the home
    payment is the suspicious one (a copy of the card used while they travel);
  - a country the notice does not name, or a gap too short for any flight: the notice explains nothing.
- Typical actions: block the card, contact the customer to confirm whether they hold the card and travelled.
- Regulations: card payments are largely outside the EUPG's duty and liability sections. Search only if
  a specific obligation is in question.
